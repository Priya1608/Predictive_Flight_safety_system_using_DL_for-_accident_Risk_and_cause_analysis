Approach Phase

The phase of flight starting from the initial descent down to the flare. Critical factors analyzed include altitude, speed, pitch, vertical speed, and weather conditions to detect anomalies like CFIT, Stalls, or Engine Failures.

The final critical phase where the aircraft touches down and decelerates. The engine evaluates vertical speed at touchdown, crosswind components, and runway conditions to predict Hard Landings or Runway Excursions.

About Flight Anomalies & Safety Ranges

Safe vs. Danger Ranges

Vertical Speed: A safe descent is typically between -100 and -800 ft/min. Dangerously high sink rates (e.g., -1500 ft/min) often lead to hard landings or CFIT.
Pitch: Normal pitch ranges from -2.0° to +5.0°. Extreme nose-down or nose-up attitudes can result in stalls or terrain impacts.
Speed: Approach speeds should generally be 130-150 knots. Speeds significantly lower risk stalls, while higher speeds risk runway excursions.
Weather & Visibility: Safe visibility is above 5km. Storms, heavy fog (vis < 2km), and high crosswinds (>20 knots) drastically increase risk factors.

Model Classifications (8 Scenarios)

  • Safe Approach: All parameters nominal during descent.
  • CFIT (Approach): Controlled Flight Into Terrain. Often associated with poor visibility, extremely low altitude, and negative pitch.
  • Stall (Approach): A loss of lift caused by exceeding the critical angle of attack (low speed, high pitch).
  • Engine Failure (Approach): Loss of primary thrust during descent.
  • Safe Landing: All parameters nominal during final touchdown.
  • Hard Landing (Landing): Occurs when the aircraft strikes the runway with excessive vertical force (-800+ ft/min).
  • Runway Excursion (Landing): Aircraft veers off runway. Triggered by excessive speed, crosswinds, and poor surface conditions.
  • Engine Failure (Landing): Loss of primary thrust during final landing flare.
